Section 1 - Summary

SMC Resources is a curated online resource platform designed to help students prepare for high school mathematics competitions by organizing the best existing resources into one structured, easy-to-navigate website. Rather than creating new problems or functioning as an AI tutor, the website serves as a centralized directory of a wide variety of high-quality contest math materials collected from the internet.
The platform focuses on four core areas of contest mathematics:
Number Theory
Geometry
Combinatorics
Algebra
Each subject is organized into two preparation tracks:
AIME Qualifier Materials – covering the highest-yield topics for students preparing for the AMC 10/12 and aiming to qualify for the AIME.
Advanced Contest Materials– covering more difficult material intended for students pursuing Distinguished Honor Roll, high AIME scores, and advanced high school competitions such as HMMT, PUMaC, SMT, MMATHS, CMIMC, and ARML.

Each of the four main pages will have a large centered (everything besides this will be left aligned) header saying the topic (one of number theory, geometry, algebra or combinatorics)
Underneath that will be a slightly smaller header saying  “aime qualifier materials” and below this is a description of the track. For Aime Qualifier Materials the description will be “Build the core skills needed for strong AMC 10/12 performance and AIME qualification.”  Underneath this will be several topics. In each of those topics will be links to handouts. 
This format repeats for the Advanced Contest Track except the description will be “Develop deeper problem-solving skills for challenging AMC, AIME, and invitational contest problems.”


Section 2 - Project Goals
The primary goals of the project are:
Organize high-quality contest math resources into a single, structured platform.
Reduce the amount of time students spend searching across dozens of websites for reliable materials.
Create a logical learning path for students at different stages of contest preparation.
Make excellent but lesser-known handouts, mock contests, and practice resources easier to discover.
Encourage consistent practice through curated recommendations rather than overwhelming students with hundreds of unorganized links.
Provide an intuitive interface that allows students to quickly locate resources relevant to their current needs.
Maintain a clean, modern, and professional website that is enjoyable to browse for extended study sessions.
Create a platform that can continue growing as new resources become available while remaining easy to maintain.

Section 3 - Non-Goals
To clearly define the project's scope, the website is not intended to be:
An AI tutoring platform.
A problem-solving website where users submit answers.
An online judge or grading system.
A replacement for existing contest archives.
A repository of original contest problems.
Instead, the website complements existing resources by organizing, categorizing, and presenting them in a significantly more accessible way.

Section 4 - Target Users
Primary Audience
The primary audience consists of members of the WWP High School South Math Club preparing for mathematics competitions.
Students will have a wide range of experience levels, from newcomers studying for their first AMC 10 to experienced competitors preparing for the AIME, USA(J)MO qualification, Distinguished Honor Roll, and invitational competitions.
The website should accommodate all of these students through clear organization and separate preparation tracks.

Secondary Audience
The website should also be useful for:
High school students from other schools preparing for math competitions.
Math circle participants.
Coaches and teachers looking for organized teaching materials.
Parents seeking high-quality contest preparation resources for their children.
The design should therefore remain general enough that it can be adopted by a broader audience without feeling tied exclusively to one school.

Section 5 - Visual Design Specification
Overall Design Philosophy
The website should have a clean, modern, documentation-style aesthetic that prioritizes readability and organization over flashy visuals. The design should feel like a polished academic resource rather than a social media site or commercial startup. Users should be able to browse hundreds of resources without feeling overwhelmed.
The interface should emphasize whitespace, consistency, and simple navigation. Every page should feel lightweight and uncluttered.

Section 6 - Color Palette
The overall interface should use a predominantly white color scheme.
Primary background: White
Secondary background: Very light gray for section separation and cards
Primary text: Dark charcoal (near black)
Primary accent: Deep forest green
Secondary accent: Gold (used sparingly)
The approximate visual balance should be:
80% neutral (white/light gray)
15% green
5–10% gold
Gold should not be used for navigation or primary buttons. Instead, it should be subtly included on the cards

Section 7 - Typography
Use a clean, modern sans-serif font throughout the website.
Typography hierarchy:
Large bold page titles
Medium section headings
Smaller topic headings
Comfortable body text
Clearly distinguishable links
Large page titles should remain dark charcoal rather than green to maximize readability.

Section 8 - General Layout
